Why This Mattered Architecturally

1. Coverage pressure broke intent into surfaces

What started as broad “oak-chain” intent was forced into handler, router, Aeron, and upcoming GC surfaces with concrete contracts.

2. The router stopped being magic

Once routes were test-driven, RequestRouter became a readable map of the system rather than a vague dispatch blob.

3. Aeron became inspectable

Degraded cluster state, stale cache fallback, follower lookup, and leadership history turned from hidden runtime assumptions into testable behavior.

4. The v1 boundary sharpened

Deliverables stayed in scope while oak-auth-web3 and oak-segment-agentic were deliberately pushed out of the gate.

5. The next leverage point moved

The main drag is no longer browser or dashboard churn. The leverage is now GC proposal, payment, debt, and execution policy code.

V1 Boundary

The merge gate is coverage plus hard load testing, not coverage alone. The scope boundary is part of the discipline: only the real deliverables should consume the remaining energy.
